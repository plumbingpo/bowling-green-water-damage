Typical Water Damage Problems in Elizabethtown

Walk through a house from the roof down and you pass most of the places a leak can begin. That is the route we take when inspecting a property in Elizabethtown, and five spots along it account for most calls.

Attics: The top of the house takes on water when shingles are damaged or the seal around a vent pipe gives out. Insulation acts like a sponge, so it can hold a surprising amount before any of it shows on the ceiling. Wet insulation stops insulating and keeps the wood beneath it damp. Our part is removing what is saturated and drying the wood, while a roofing contractor closes the opening.

Bathrooms: A tub left filling, a sink with a blocked overflow hole or a toilet that runs over can soak a bathroom floor quickly. Even a tiled floor lets water pass at the grout, the tub edge and the base of the walls. In an upstairs bathroom the next stop is the ceiling of the room underneath. Anything that came out of the toilet bowl should be treated as dirty water and cleaned with disinfectant.

Walls: Plumbing hidden in a wall may seep at a joint for weeks without leaving a puddle anywhere. You might see trim pulling away, a discolored spot spreading or drywall that gives when pressed. On these calls in Elizabethtown, an infrared camera and a pin meter show us how far the dampness reaches. Only that section is opened and dried, once a plumber has stopped the leak.

Tanks: The hot water tank sits out of sight in a garage, closet or utility space and stays full all the time. Worn parts announce themselves with moisture at the top connections, the drain spout or the pressure relief pipe. When the tank itself fails, close the cold water valve above it and switch off the gas or power to the unit.
